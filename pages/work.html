CASE 001
Prototype delivered · version one in scoping

The Territory Brief.

A national sales organisation that could not see its own numbers before a customer meeting. Five screens, one daily refresh, and a brief written from the rows themselves.

The ask

Allied Group and Hackney Ladish sell pipe fittings and valves, including into data-centre builds. Their ERP is called 4U. It is too slow to open when a rep is sitting in a truck outside a branch, so the number the rep walks in with is whatever they remember from last month.

The brief asked for a web dashboard for every rep nationwide: data from the daily 4U drop, each rep scoped to their own territory, current sales and weights, quarter-on-quarter and year-on-year movement, colour-coded so problem areas are obvious, and no manual updates from the sales team. The stated success test was one sentence: a rep clicks through in about twenty minutes and knows their territory before a meeting.

What was built

A five-screen prototype — overview, quarter and year comparisons, accounts, an AI brief, and follow-ups — running on 11,232 rows of synthetic data shaped to their world: eight territories, PVF distributors and contractors, data-centre builders, six product lines sold by weight. Territory scoping is enforced by role: the national view sees the roll-up, a rep sees one territory and cannot switch out of it.

Every number on every screen is computed from the ingested rows, so what a client clicks is what version one would do. The AI brief is generated from those rows, not from a general model guessing.

How it was verified

  • A real headless browser session drove the prototype: 20 checks, 0 failures
  • Every territory's year-to-date and year-on-year figure on screen compared against an independently computed ground truth
  • Every view opened by clicking, every question chip exercised, the role lock and the follow-up toggle tested, console errors asserted to be zero
  • Two real defects found this way and fixed: a period filter that silently returned an empty quarter, and a formatter that crashed on an aggregate object. Neither was visible in a screenshot.

The demo

The prototype is public and runs on made-up numbers shaped like the real ones. It takes about twenty minutes to click through, which is the client's own standard.

What version one adds

The same five screens wired to a real drop: format-agnostic ingestion with validation and alerting, per-rep accounts with server-side territory scoping, an unattended daily refresh that keeps the last good day live when a drop is late, rollout to every rep, and a handover runbook. Eight weeks, phased, pilot in week five.

What is not proven yet

No real 4U data has been read — that is week one of the project and it is deliberately the first paid step. The prototype was verified in one browser engine; mobile was checked at the narrowest width the test harness exposes, not on a physical device. Delivery time is a plan, not a measured build.
